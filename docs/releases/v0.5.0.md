# Ono-Sendai v0.5.0 — time as a coordinate, and evidence as the limit of what may be said

This document is written while the tranche is being built, and it is honest about that: v0.5.0 is
**in progress**. `docs/ACCEPTANCE.md` §4.11 is its definition of done, derived from the
specification's own forty-seven acceptance scenarios, its performance evidence, its twenty core
invariants and its twenty-three release criteria. Every box that subsection still holds open is
enumerated below, because §66.9 permits an open box only as a recorded exclusion and §66.8 asks
these notes to say so. A reader learns what is unfinished here or nowhere.

`docs/specs/ono_sendai_shell_spec_v0.5_temporal_causal_systems_interface.md` is the specification.

> Space made the machine explorable. Time makes it alive — as far as the evidence reaches, and
> no further.

## What the tranche is

v0.4 gave the session a place. v0.5 gives it an instant. `at -10m` moves the temporal coordinate
and leaves the spatial one alone; `now` comes back. Between those two words sit an evidence
ledger, a reconstruction engine, a timeline, a `changes` engine and a causal engine, and one rule
that governs all of them: Ono reconstructs only what its evidence supports, and a nearby event is
never promoted to a cause.

Three decisions the specification could not make for itself are recorded as ADRs, because each
collides with something already shipped:

- **ADR-0610** — v0.5 §34 numbers the temporal error family E1101–E1114, and v0.4.1 §21.4 had
  already spent the first three of those on the resource family. The names §34 fixes are kept
  exactly; the codes take E1301–E1314, the lowest free block. A reader holding §34 open adds two
  hundred.
- **ADR-0611** — v0.5 §35 asks for `ono.evidence/1`, which v0.2 §31.24 already holds and which
  carries an unrelated record. The temporal one is registered as `ono.temporal-evidence/1`.
- **ADR-0612** — the six crates of §39, their places in the layering, and the reference store.

## Structure

Six crates, as §39 fixes them:

- `ono-temporal-core` — the canonical vocabulary: event, evidence, coverage, gap, causal link,
  time selector, temporal context, and the ledger contract itself. It names no SQL.
- `ono-temporal-ledger` — the bounded in-memory ledger and the SQLite WAL store beneath it, with
  migrations, retention, integrity and source-sequence continuity.
- `ono-temporal-reconstruct` — checkpoints, event replay, coverage composition, gap propagation.
- `ono-temporal-query` — timeline, changes, event search, causal planning, relevance.
- `ono-temporal-render` — presentation only. It depends on `ono-value` and on nothing else, so
  §39.3's rule that a renderer reaches no provider, ledger or network is a fact about the
  dependency graph rather than a habit.
- `ono-recorder` — the user-level, opt-in, bounded collection service.

`ono-cli` parses, dispatches, and owns the active temporal context. It is not the engine.

## Not yet proven

### 4.11.1 Contracts and vocabulary (T1, §34, §35, §36)

- This subsection is held to the tree.
- The twelve configuration settings of §33 are typed, inspectable and default as specified.

### 4.11.2 Temporal context (§4, §12, §48.1 scenarios 1–6)

- `at -1m` enters historical context and the prompt says so.
- `now` returns to the present and keeps the place when it still exists.
- An invalid or ambiguous time leaves the context untouched.
- A native mutation in past context answers `temporal.read_only`.
- The read-only rule covers KUANG/11 and remote mutations too.
- An arbitrary external command in past context answers `temporal.present_only`.
- `present printf ok` executes and preserves the historical coordinate.
- A pure Ono transform over reconstructed values still runs in past context.
- `--at` uses the same engine as `at`.
- Temporal movement keeps its own trail and leaves `back` alone.

### 4.11.3 Timeline, events and discovery (§11, §20, §28, §48.2 scenarios 7–12)

- A process appearing is a typed event.
- The process exiting is a disappearance carrying the same lifetime identity.
- `timeline` at a service place excludes the firehose by default.
- `timeline --all` widens to the visible scope.
- `timeline` is pipeline-compatible.
- An event reference is stable, inspectable and usable.
- A recorder downtime interval is rendered as a gap.
- `find event` answers a predicate without a timestamp being known.
- Historical context centres the default timeline on the coordinate.
- `now()` and `context.time` are different things and both are documented.
- Command history and the temporal ledger stay separate.

### 4.11.4 Reconstruction and coverage (§9, §42, §48.3 scenarios 13–18)

- A checkpoint plus later change reconstructs the earlier service state.
- A PID reused later does not resolve as the historical process.
- A relation added after `T` does not appear in a map at `T`.
- Absence is reported only where coverage can support it.
- Sparse evidence produces unknown, never fake completeness.
- A historical collection says whether it is the complete set.
- History outside retention answers `temporal.out_of_retention`.
- A reconstructed object keeps its own schema and gains temporal metadata.
- Coverage is composed per capability, never as one global label.
- A place that did not exist at `T` says so and stays where it is.

### 4.11.5 Changes (§13, §48.4 scenarios 19–21)

- `changes --since` reports added, removed and changed objects with typed values.
- A partial before-state is unknown, never zero or empty.
- v0.4 `look`'s recent-change section is backed by the temporal engine.
- `changes` is pipeline-compatible.

### 4.11.6 Causality (§15, §16, §17, §26, §48.5 scenarios 22–27)

- An Ono `restart service` creates an ActionId and records the systemd job it became.
- `why` on the resulting transition returns a causal chain.
- A config change shortly before a failure is correlation and stays correlation.
- A preceding unrelated event never appears under `known cause`.
- Unknown cause is a successful typed explanation.
- Every causal edge names its rule, source and evidence.
- `why` works without a model configured.
- `why` refuses ambiguity rather than choosing.
- The three forms of `why` all work.
- Temporal proximity alone never produces `caused_by`.
- The renderer never uses causal wording for a non-causal edge.
- An AI hypothesis cannot become a canonical causal edge.

### 4.11.7 Rewind, the temporal HUD and the full-screen timeline (§18, §19, §48.6 scenarios 28–33)

- `map --live` pauses without stopping ingestion.
- Stepping backward moves the topology to the previous significant event.
- Stepping into a coverage gap shows the gap.
- Enter on a past map node enters that historical place.
- `N` returns to now and summarises what changed.
- Terminal resize during the temporal view changes no semantic time or place.
- The full-screen timeline opens, navigates and exits cleanly.
- Dense event sets are grouped with their hidden counts and time spans preserved.
- No frame is interpolated between unsupported states.

### 4.11.8 Recorder, storage, privacy (§10, §30, §31, §43, §44, §48.7 scenarios 34–39)

- The recorder is disabled by default.
- `start recorder` creates a private ledger with the specified permissions.
- Events survive a shell restart.
- A recorder restart marks its downtime as a gap.
- Retention expires old data and leaves no dangling reference.
- A secret never appears in the ledger's bytes.
- Process argv is not persisted by default.
- A corrupted ledger disables the affected history and leaves the shell working.
- The store migrates, and every shipped version's fixture is tested.
- The recorder gains no visibility the user does not already have.
- Every ingestion path is bounded and an overflow becomes a gap.
- `remove temporal-history` clears the local store under destructive-operation policy.
- The in-memory session ledger is bounded and its eviction is visible.

### 4.11.9 Remote and distributed time (§24, §25, §26, §48.8 scenarios 40–43)

- Remote source time and local ingest time stay distinct.
- Clock skew alone never creates a causal edge.
- Explicit cross-host transaction evidence may create a causal chain.
- A remote host with no history says so.
- Temporal capability negotiation reports what the peer has.
- Clock uncertainty is rendered where it matters.
- A remote mutation from historical context is refused at the agent.

### 4.11.10 KUANG/11 temporal extensions (§30.7, §37, §48.9 scenarios 44–47)

- A plugin without `temporal.read.history` cannot query retained history.
- A plugin without `temporal.contribute.causality` cannot add a causal link.
- A plugin's correlation remains correlation in core rendering.
- A contributed event outside the permitted spatial scope is rejected.
- Contributed events are validated for schema, identity, timestamps, size and rate.
- A malformed plugin temporal payload is refused without destabilising the host.

### 4.11.11 Performance and scale (§32, §49)

- A deterministic fixture ledger of the declared cardinality exists and builds.
- Startup with temporal persistence disabled is measured and within budget.
- Recorder idle overhead is measured.
- The eight measurements of §49 are recorded against the fixture.
- v0.4's current-state budgets do not regress.
- A long historical query is cancellable and Ctrl-C leaves no lock held.
- Retention cleanup runs as bounded background work.

### 4.11.12 The twenty core invariants (§2)

- Time is explicit.
- The past is read-only.
- History requires evidence.
- Absence is not default knowledge.
- Coverage is first-class.
- Gaps remain gaps.
- Causation is stricter than correlation.
- Ordering is not causation.
- Clock uncertainty is visible.
- Provenance survives reconstruction.
- Live and historical views share one truth.
- System history and shell history are distinct.
- Ono actions are traceable.
- External commands remain honest.
- Retention is bounded.
- Recording is opt-in.
- Recording does not escalate privilege.
- Secrets stay out of history.
- Machine-readable semantics precede rendering.
- No fake rewind.

### 4.11.13 Release criteria (§56) and delivery

- Every normative temporal command is machine-registered.
- The shell is unchanged when temporal persistence is disabled.
- `ono-cli` is not the temporal engine.
- The temporal documentation of §46 exists.
- The fuzz targets of §47.3 exist and run.
- The acceptance container enables the recorder for the temporal cases.
- `scripts/release-check.sh` is green.
